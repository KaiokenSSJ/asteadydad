Nemesis by Isaac Azimov

I picked up this book randomly from my partner’s bookshelf. It had been sitting there for years, and I wanted something short, so I thought, “Why not?” Unfortunately, I ended up pretty disappointed.

An ending doomed from the start

Halfway through, I was already frustrated. I had a bad feeling the ending would be either far-fetched or way too rushed, considering how many pages were left and how little had happened. In the end, that’s exactly what happened.

Bland Characters

The characters felt flat to me. They lacked personality and drive. I’m not sure if it was their motivations or just the slow development, but I couldn’t connect with any of them.

Marlene Insigna:

I found her very uninteresting. Her entire personality is described through other people’s eyes—either focusing on her appearance or on their discomfort with her ability to read them.

The problem is, her power is so over-the-top that it feels like a superpower rather than a believable skill. For someone who’s supposed to be so important, she felt empty.

Crile Fisher:

He’s a central character with logical motivations, but he has no soul. He takes up way too many chapters for a result that was… well, not worth it.

Janus Pitt:

He was actually the only interesting character to me. But he was underused and pushed aside too quickly. The story would’ve had much more depth if he had played a bigger role.

Also, without spoiling anything, a character appears late in the book and suddenly takes center stage, but we don’t get enough time to actually care about him and his story.

Sci-Fi vs. Politics & Society

The whole mystery about the planet Nemesis felt almost useless. It didn’t add much to the plot. Honestly, I think the book would’ve been better if it had focused entirely on politics and space conquest instead of the sci-fi elements.

Final Thoughts

The writing style was nice and easy to follow, though the planet and orbit mechanics were a bit confusing for someone not familiar with that stuff.

That said, between the rushed ending and the wasted characters, it’s hard to say I enjoyed this read.

It had potential, but Asimov tried to tie up too many storylines at once.
